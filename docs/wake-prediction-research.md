# Predicting the optimal wake time: what the evidence supports and what to build next

Written before any real night was recorded. Every claim is either literature with a URL, a fact read out of this repo's code, or arithmetic marked as mine. Where the research threads disagree, or where a claim is weak, it says so.

## 2. What "optimal wake time" means, and what the evidence supports

**Sleep inertia is large but brief.** Impairment on waking can match performance after up to 40 h of sleep deprivation; most measures recover in 15-30 min, full recovery takes at least an hour, and in one study subjective alertness improved for ~2 h [1]. A rating at breakfast measures only the tail — and the two kinds of measure dissociate: after a 30-min nap participants rated themselves better while performing worse [1].

**Stage at awakening: plausible, not established.** For: a 41% performance drop after slow-wave awakenings versus none after N2, and decision-making at 51% of optimum after SWS against 65% after REM [55]. Against: Lovato found no impairment though 91% woke from SWS, and at least four other studies found no stage link [1]. The replicated half is narrower than the smart-alarm pitch: **waking out of deep sleep is the bad case**, not that some light stage is good. And little N3 is left at 6:30, since deep sleep concentrates in the first third while REM dominates the final third [4] — so if the detector's job is avoiding N3 it has little to do.

**Circadian phase has an effect, but not one this window can measure.** Scheer's forced-desynchrony study (n=12, seven 28-h cycles) found an endogenous rhythm in sleep inertia, worst in the 300° circadian bin, which the authors place at "~2300-0300 h in these subjects" [2]. Phase 0° is the fitted core-body-temperature minimum, so 300° is about 4 h *before* CBTmin; with CBTmin roughly 1.5-2 h before habitual wake (a soft number — the peer-reviewed offset could not be pinned down and the "1.5 ± 1.0 h" figure traces to a patent filing), the worst bin sits 5.5-6 h before a 06:30 wake, near 00:30. My arithmetic: across the whole 0-90 min window phase moves only ~22°, and Scheer reports "a lack of a circadian variation in cognitive performance 20 min after awakening" [2]. Correcting an earlier draft: the effect is **not** shown independent of background performance (each subject was normalised to his own mean and adjusted for a practice trend), and the paper gives **no measured penalty across a span this short**. So the case for a floor on how early the window fires is not a circadian penalty. It is that a floor caps worst-case sleep truncation on a short night and bounds the experiment to a range where one arm can plausibly differ from the other.

**Sleep loss amplifies inertia more reliably than stage does.** Restriction (5.6 h vs 8 h) produced a 10% immediate drop unrecovered at 70 min [1], so duration and a rolling deficit probably predict the morning better than any stage label. Cycle length will not: 369 participants and 6,064 PSG-scored cycles give a median of 96 min, non-normal, with lengthening that varies with sleep pressure [3] — fixed "multiple of 90 min" arithmetic is out.

**The arousal base rate could fail in either direction.** Scored awakenings in normal subjects averaged 4/h slept (95% CI 1-15) and ASDA arousals 21/h (7-56) [5]; my arithmetic puts ~2 awakenings and ~10 brief arousals in a 30-min window, so the plausible failure is firing the instant the window opens. Against that, movement-only scoring has wake specificity of 0.34-0.68 — from 35 recruited healthy volunteers, 22 satisfactory PSG nights of which only 9 were adults [23] — so it may equally miss most of them. **The base rate is unknown and is the first thing the recorded nights should report.**

**Smart alarms: no efficacy evidence anywhere.** Fitbit documents exactly this design — start 30 min early, avoid deep sleep, fall back to the set time — with no published validation against PSG or morning alertness [6], and none was found for the other consumer apps. The closest controlled test found "little overall impact", with moderate evening types given ≤15 min of light showing *more* PVT lapses than the control alarm [7].

**Two things have better evidence than stage detection.** Dawn simulation: 30 min ramping to 300 lux significantly reduced subjective sleepiness and raised activation in 16 difficult wakers — selected for needing ≥60 min to wake, which made them relatively late chronotypes (mean midsleep on free days 5:53) — with no effect on performance, core temperature or cortisol [8]. And self-awakening: in studies of ~10-11 participants forced awakening degraded reaction time while self-awakening did not [9]. That makes **"woke before the buzz" the headline outcome**, and the alarm a safety net whose job is to fire less often over time.

**Regularity.** The Sleep Regularity Index beat sleep duration for all-cause mortality in 60,977 UK Biobank participants (most- vs least-regular quintile HR 0.70; adding duration did not improve fit, p=.20), with the gradient flattening above the median of 81 [11]. For next-morning alertness the evidence is thinner: in 59 undergraduates with usable grades, GPA correlated with the SD of sleep onset at r=-0.43 (p<0.001) but with the SD of wake time only at r=-0.29 (p=0.02) [12]. An earlier draft added that "a smaller sample found no wake-time effect at all"; no source was ever found for it — unconfirmed, ignore it. **Wake-time consistency matters less than bedtime consistency**, and here it will be compressed by the alarm itself, so onset SD is the metric with real variance. Keep both SD-type and consecutive-day-type metrics [14]; one diary boolean adds social jetlag [13].

**What this implies.** Cap how early the window fires, make its length adaptive to sleep debt, keep the hard fallback and the 10-minute buzz cap. The snooze evidence cuts both ways, and the halves are different papers: 31 habitual snoozers showed no harm to sleep, cognition, mood, cortisol or overall sleep architecture from a 30-min snooze [10], while a separate PSG study of 10 students found snooze alarms roughly tripled sleep-stage transitions in the last 20 min of sleep (M=12.2, SE 2.0 vs 3.5, SE 0.92; t(9)=3.538, p=0.006) [62]. And do not oversell the heart-rate rule: circadian anticipation is real [57][60], but cortisol is not collectable here and no study was found measuring a heart-rate *lead* on spontaneous awakening. **The HR rule detects an arousal; it does not predict one.**

## 3. What the current data can and cannot tell

**A correction to the brief.** The 10-second row carries no mean heart rate. `pilog.boot.js` keeps the single *most confident* reading (`if(h.bpm>0&&h.confidence>=hc)`), and `hr_conf` describes that same reading. The `HRM` event fires **once per detected beat** — `hrm_new` returns `hrm_had_beat()`, so it is beat-driven, not on a 1 s timer; at a resting 52-60 bpm that lands near one per second, which is where the earlier "about once a second" came from [63]. So roughly nine readings per row are discarded, including their spread, itself an arousal feature. Three more limits read out of the code:

- **`powerSave:false` does not set the poll interval.** The logger never calls `Bangle.setPollInterval(80)`, and the docs state that `powerSave:false` will *not* change the interval from its current value [25]. A night can be logged at 1.25 Hz instead of 12.5 Hz; `samples` is the check (~125 per row when correct, ~12 when not).
- **`ax/ay/az` are rounded to two decimals.** At 1 g, 0.01 g is ~0.57°, so posture (15°) is safe; breathing-induced tilt is not. The validated accelerometer-respiration method works on gravity-vector rotation angles [33]. Four decimals is the precondition for respiration.
- **`mov_sum`/`mov_max` destroy the breathing band.** Breathing at 0.2-0.33 Hz is well inside 12.5 Hz sampling, but summing |diff| over 10 s is one-way.

**Spectral and accuracy ceilings.** My arithmetic: one value per 10 s means Nyquist 0.05 Hz, so of the standard HRV bands (VLF 0.003-0.04, LF 0.04-0.15, HF 0.15-0.4 Hz [20]) VLF is recoverable, the bottom quarter of LF is, and **HF — the respiratory/parasympathetic band — is not**. Walch's classifier sits inside the limit: HR interpolated to 1 s, difference-of-Gaussians (σ₁=120 s, σ₂=600 s), per-person normalised, rolling SD over 10 min [15]. On 31 subjects the neural net with motion + HR gave ~90% sleep/wake accuracy with **59.6% wake specificity** at its 93%-sleep-sensitivity row (κ 0.449; the Discussion quotes κ 0.455, which is the 95% row), and 3-class wake/NREM/REM 72.3% accuracy at **κ 0.256** [15]. Beattie (60 adults) got 4-class 69% and κ 0.52 [17]; Fonseca got sleep/wake κ 0.55 with HRV + accel against 0.40 for actigraphy alone [18]. Beattie also found κ roughly doubles when heart-rate features join motion features [17] — the case for keeping the HRM on — and Oura claims 57% to 79% on 4-stage, cross-validated inside one dataset [54]; an earlier draft's "independent validations landing at 76.3%" could not be confirmed in any source, so drop it. **No threshold tuning here yields a trustworthy hypnogram; commit to a binary "is he arousing now".**

**The Detector's rules, with numbers.** `active_floor` 10.0 movement/min or `active_ratio` 4.0 × resting movement (20th percentile since onset); `posture_degrees` 15° between adjacent minutes' gravity vectors; `hr_rise_bpm` 8.0 for 2 consecutive minutes above a rolling 60-minute median. One external comparison, with care about the units. The firmware emits `health.movement` as `health->movement / health->movementSamples` over `accDiff`, where `8192 = 1g` — a *mean* per-sample |diff| in g×8192 units [59]; that unit is in the firmware, not in `sleeplog`'s README. My arithmetic converts a threshold T to this project's scale (mean |diff| × 750, assuming both at 12.5 Hz) as T × 0.0916. The upstream app disagrees with itself: its README gives deep/light thresholds of 100/200 → **9.2 and 18.3**, while `boot.js` and `settings.js` default to `deepTh: 150`, `lightTh: 300` → **13.7 and 27.5** [30]. So `active_floor` 10.0 sits at or below `sleeplog`'s deep/light boundary depending on which the app means — either way, as an absolute floor it calls anything above deep sleep "active". The adaptive 4× term should dominate; confirm from the first nights that 4 × `resting_mov` > 10. The reference `sleepphasealarm` app is scaled differently again: ~1 s windows, non-moving below 0.023 g SD of accel magnitude [29].

**One research thread is wrong about the HR rule, in the project's favour.** It wants detrending because a fixed +8 bpm rule "gets progressively easier to trip toward morning". That holds for a whole-night baseline, not here: `_update_baselines` keeps a *trailing* 60-minute median, so drift is absorbed. The real failure is the opposite — a rise spread over more than an hour is invisible — so add a slow feature (HR slope over the last 60 min) rather than replacing the trailing window.

**What beat intervals would add, and the cheap route to them.** RMSSD, SDNN and LF power are lowest in slow-wave sleep while heart rate is highest in REM — opposite on HRV, overlapping on mean HR, which is why mean bpm confuses the two [21]; a SHAP analysis of 1,743 MESA participants found frequency-domain cardiac features the most critical staging inputs [19]. **The cheapest route needs no new event stream.** Every plain `HRM` event already carries a `history` array: `hrm_get_hrm_info()` pushes `HRM_HIST_LEN = 16` beat-derived BPM values onto the event object, and `jswrap_bangle.c` calls it on every `JSBT_HRM_DATA` dispatch [63][59]. The underlying `hrmInfo.times[]` are previous beat intervals stored as `uint8_t` in 1/100 s, so recoverable timing resolution is **10 ms** — four times finer than the 40 ms the `HRM-raw` route lives with — at zero extra power and no poll-interval change; intervals are clamped to 255 (2.55 s) and rejected below 20 [63]. `HRM-raw` stays the fallback: per-sample `raw`/`filt` at a default 40 ms (25 Hz), settable via `hrmPollInterval` [26], with 2× interpolation before peak detection as `hrvtracking` does [27]. One caution on both: the Bangle's beat timing has never been validated against ECG, only minute-averaged HR (CCC 0.78 vs Polar H10, n=47) — and that protocol was a treadmill at 2-5 mph, stair climbing and 24 h of free living [32], i.e. motion conditions. CCC 0.78 is therefore neither a floor nor a ceiling for a still overnight wrist, plausibly the sensor's best case, and the first nights can measure it since `hr_conf` is already logged.

## 4. Factors to add from data already collected

No new hardware. All computable from the night CSV, `wake-DATE.json` and `diary.json`.

**4.1 Wake-time consistency and the Sleep Regularity Index.** `analyze()` already produces per-minute `awake` flags plus `onset`/`final_wake` — the binary state vector SRI needs. The Methods settle the convention secondary sources are ambiguous about: SRI is "the likelihood that any two time-points (minute-by-minute) 24 h apart were the same sleep/wake state, across all days", rescaled `y = 200(x − 1/2)` to −100..100 [12]. Same epoch, next day, 1-minute epochs:

```
SRI = -100 + (200 / (M·(N-1))) · Σ_j Σ_i δ(s[i,j], s[i,j+1])
      M = 1440 epochs, N consecutive days
      δ = 1 when the state at epoch i on day j matches the same epoch on day j+1
```

Two requirements from the same Methods. "Only pairs of non-missing time-points were used" — so epochs inside a `LONG_GAP_SECONDS` gap, or with no recording, drop from both the numerator and the pair count rather than scoring as awake. And the reported SRI "was computed using the longest interval of a whole number of weeks with no missing data to avoid day-of-week bias (i.e., a time interval that is a multiple of 7 days)" [12] — which matters because §4.7 adds a work-day/free-day distinction. **Nights:** 8 for a first value, 14 for stability; target ~80, not 100 [11]. Track **SD of sleep onset** over 14 days separately — stronger association (r=-0.43 vs -0.29) [12], unaffected by the alarm, and the only timing the owner controls.

**Which consistency metrics reach the decision.** SRI and onset SD are **display only** — the Sleep card, plus a bedtime nudge the system does not yet have. What enters the Detector is the **trailing 14-night median of `final_wake` and the SD around it**, because those set and weight the personal-wake cosine in §4.3: `wake_anchor = median(final_wake, 14n)`, `wake_sd_14d = SD(final_wake, 14n)`, and the cosine's weight tapers linearly from full weight at a tight anchor to zero at a loose one, both break points fit from the first month rather than guessed now. `onset_sd_14d` is the second gate: if bedtime is scattered, tonight's phase estimate is unreliable however tight the alarm has made wake times look. Three fields gate one feature: `wake_anchor`, `wake_sd_14d`, `onset_sd_14d`.

**4.2 Sleep debt.** `debt = asleep_minutes - median(trailing 14 nights)`. On a night more than ~60 min short, shrink or skip the window rather than truncating sleep further — restriction alone cost 10% immediate performance, unrecovered at 70 min [1].

**4.3 Clock proxy / circadian anchor.** `time_since_onset` plus the §4.1 cosine: the cheapest improvement available. In Walch's neural net, adding a clock proxy to motion + HR moved 3-class κ from 0.256 to 0.277 (Table 6) and balanced-threshold NREM accuracy from 0.622 to 0.651 (same table, at the threshold holding wake-correct at 0.6), and improved the fraction of wake epochs scored correctly "by about 14%" at fixed 90% sleep sensitivity — the paper does not say whether that is relative or absolute, and on the same analysis MESA's wake specificity rose about 5% [15]. A better anchor later is the **HR nadir time**: against the MEQ chronotype questionnaire, cosinor acrophases correlated at r=-0.73 for heart rate and -0.71 for mean RR, against -0.71 for the Actigraph reference activity and -0.66 for the same watch's activity, and -0.61 for core temperature [58]. HR is therefore **as good as activity and free**, not "the strongest wearable marker" — and these are correlations with a questionnaire, not measured circadian phase; the paper's best model (R²=0.64) combined watch activity, core and skin temperature and three cardiac acrophases.

**4.4 Cycle-phase estimate — record, do not act on.** Estimate the dominant periodicity per night by autocorrelation or FFT of the per-minute `mov` and `hr` series over a 40-140 min band, and store it. HDCZA, the best-validated onset estimator, is still ~31 min off against PSG [22], and against 96-min non-normal cycles [3] the feature carries more error than signal.

**4.5 Awakenings.** `awake_spans` gives count, total WASO, longest awakening and the last awakening before the window, free. Use them as covariates, and the count to sanity-check the detector against ~4 awakenings/h and ~21 arousals/h [5].

**4.6 Heart-rate trend in the last hour.** (i) **Walch's HR feature** [15], as above — published and validated for this sensor pair, where the current `+8 bpm` rule has nothing behind it. (ii) **Oura's Recovery Index** [49]: hours asleep after the HR nadir and the nadir's position in the night, with Oura's reading (≥6 h after the nadir optimal, nadir in the first half better) giving it meaning before any personal data exists. (iii) **HR slope over the last 60 min**.

**4.7 Diary covariates.** Keep melatonin, sober and notes. Add a **work-day/free-day boolean**, which alone unlocks social jetlag [13], and **chronotype once** in `settings.json`, since chronotype was the moderator in the one randomised smart-alarm trial [7]. Split the 1-10 rating into one at wake and one ~30 min later [1].

**4.8 Outcome measures, and which are actually free.** From `wake-DATE.json`: **button latency** (`buzz.awake - alarm_unix`) and **resends**. From the CSV: **time to upright and moving** (first minute after the press with `mov` above `active_threshold()` sustained two minutes, plus the gravity-vector change from the sleeping posture) and **movement in the 10 min after the alarm**. None is a validated inertia proxy — no study was found testing button-press latency — so the first two weeks must establish whether they vary night to night at all.

**"Woke before the buzz" is not free.** `buzz.awake` is written only inside `_buzz` — state `BUZZING`, which begins at `alarm_unix` — and only when `status.awake >= alarm.plan.alarm_unix - AWAKE_SLACK_SECONDS`, with `AWAKE_SLACK_SECONDS = 60`. And `pilog.awake.json` is written only by `watch/src/pilog.alarm.js`, which the watch loads at alarm time. A press twenty minutes into the window is recorded nowhere, so `buzz.awake < buzz.first_sent` can only fire inside a 60-second band with the alarm screen already up. Until task 2.30 lands, report the metric in its **CSV-derived form only**: a sustained active run before `buzz.first_sent`, an inference rather than a press.

**Two buzz cadences, one name.** `buzz.resends` counts the Pi re-asking the watch every `BUZZ_RESEND_SECONDS = 120` (`wake/wake.py`) — an outcome, and a proxy for how long the button went unpressed. The watch's own repeat is `BUZZ_EVERY = 30000` in `pilog.alarm.js` and is **not logged anywhere**. The snooze literature bears on the 30 s pattern, not the 120 s re-send: it is the repeated forced awakening [62]. §6's buzz arm varies the watch value, so logging it is a prerequisite.

**Battery: the official sources disagree by a factor of seven.** The hardware page lists the HRM at 1.0 mA total, +0.7 mA over idle [24]; the `setHRMPower` reference says "roughly 5mA" [25]; the Health app README says its "Always" setting cuts battery life to ~36 hours, implying ~4.9 mA average on the 175 mAh cell [28]. My arithmetic on the first figure (accel 0.3 + BLE 0.75 + HRM 0.7 ≈ 1.75 mA) puts an 8-hour night at ~14 mAh, 8% of the pack; on the Health app figure, ~23%. **Resolve it empirically: `battery` is logged every 10 s, so one night on and one off settles it.** If it is high, copy the Health app's duty cycle (wear check, then 1-2 min of HRM every 3-10 min, off once confidence >90) outside the window and go continuous at window open [28]. Skip the magnetometer: +0.6 mA for heading adds nothing over the gravity vector [24]. Never log raw PPG all night — 25 Hz for 8 h is 720,000 samples, and `hrrawexp` caps itself at 2 hours [31].

### (b) Room sensors on the Pi

Caveats: several authors are Withings employees, and staging was weak (63%, κ 0.49), so the row endorses wake detection, not staging | Yes |
| **TSL2591** light | ~€6, I2C 0x29 | Reads from 188 µlux; the BH1750's 1 lux floor is useless in a dark bedroom [41] | Instrumentation for `sunrise.py`, not a predictor |
| **LD2410C** presence | ~$7, but see the bus | Presence and distance only, no vitals [42]. Fixes a real gap: `wake.py` cannot tell "asleep and still" from "watch on the charger", and this gives an unambiguous got-up time; no Pi library exists, so the parser is Python to write. **The real cost is the UART.** On a Pi 3B+ the good PL011 is wired to the on-board Bluetooth and the GPIO pins carry the mini-UART, whose clock comes from the VPU core clock, so its baud rate moves when the core frequency does; `enable_uart=1` pins the core at 250 MHz, and the `disable-bt` / `miniuart-bt` overlays shuffle which UART Bluetooth gets, `miniuart-bt` possibly reducing usable Bluetooth baud [65]. 256000 baud is the fragile case, and the SNOOZ and watch logger both need that Bluetooth. Three notes. Cheap CO2 sensors self-calibrate by assuming they see ~400 ppm fresh air weekly, which a bedroom violates: disable automatic self-calibration and force-calibrate at an open window every few months [40]; mounting at breathing height away from the pillow is my own judgement, not in either source. For noise, RMS-average, A-weight, log, then calibrate an offset against a reference meter (research thread, medium confidence) — the useful feature is events above the SNOOZ floor, not the absolute level. Light is the one bedroom variable with no field evidence against objective sleep: both multi-exposure studies omitted it and list it as a limitation [35][36].

**Hold off on the Seeed MR60BHA2 (~$32-37).** Seeed's own wiki says breathing and heart-rate accuracy was still in development, publishes no accuracy figure, limits vitals to a 1.5 m cone needing a 45° head-of-bed aim, and ships it as a Wi-Fi ESPHome node rather than something the Pi reads over UART [43]. Whether 60 GHz reaches the chest through a duvet is unestablished (research thread, medium confidence), and that failure mode correlates with the nights that matter most.

### (c) No hardware

**Open-Meteo**, no API key for non-commercial use: daily `sunrise`, `daylight_duration`, `sunshine_duration`; hourly `temperature_2m`, `cloud_cover`, `shortwave_radiation`; pass `timezone=auto` or daily values come back in UTC [48]. One call per night. Day length explained as much sleep-efficiency variance as humidity in the 10,903-night study (partial R² 0.08 each) [35], and cloud cover plus shortwave radiation proxy how much natural dawn reaches the room — which decides whether the LED ramp adds light or competes with it. Sunrise time also gives `sunrise.py` a seasonal anchor.

## 6. How to learn from the data

**Outcome.** Pre-commit to one primary outcome before collection starts, because subjective and objective measures dissociate and choosing afterwards is how a null becomes a positive [1]. **Primary:** a single-tap sleepiness rating on the watch at +2 min after the button press (Karolinska-style 1-9). That screen does not exist yet and nothing else here works without it, so it is task 2.31 and it ships before night 1. **Secondary:** the same at +30 min in the web diary [1]. **Objective and free:** button latency, `resends`, time-to-moving, movement in the 10 min after the alarm. "Woke before the buzz" joins the list only in its CSV-inferred form until 2.30.

**Minimal nightly feature table.** One JSONL line per night at `~/sleeptracker/data/nights.jsonl`, written by a new `analysis/nightly.py` joining the CSV, `wake-DATE.json` and `diary.json`. Rebuilding this from three sources on every analysis is where projects like this die.

Express every feature as a **z-score against his own trailing 14-day median and IQR** before it reaches a model, copying Oura's personal-baseline structure with a two-week learning period [49]. With one subject there is no population norm.

**Experiment design.** Decide the arm the evening before by coin flip, write it into `settings.json`, and copy it into `wake-DATE.json` *before* the window opens. This is a micro-randomised trial: each morning is one randomised decision point, which makes a few dozen nights an experiment rather than an observational pile, and the same machinery later answers *on which kinds of night* the window helps [51]. Use restricted alternation — never more than two mornings of the same arm in a row — so slow drift cannot read as an effect, and analyse with a **randomisation test on the recorded assignment sequence**: consecutive nights are autocorrelated (daily measures typically lag-1 0.2-0.5) and randomisation tests hold their Type I error under autocorrelation while closed-form tests do not [52].

Arms in order: detection vs fixed fade; then, once the LED exists, sunrise ramp on/off; then one continuous buzz vs the 30 s repeat, a confound inside every other experiment until settled [62]. **The sunrise arm is not independent of the detection arm.** In the dawn study the light worked partly through "the amount of wakefulness during the 30 min prior to the alarm", with arousals diverging from 5-10 min into the ramp [8] — the ramp manufactures exactly the arousal the Detector watches for. Either randomise the two as a factorial pair, or run sunrise only within the fixed-fade arm.

**Models for small data.** The "10 events per variable" rule has no real rationale and 15-50 is also argued, but the direction is clear: 30 nights with ~10 bad mornings supports roughly **one** predictor, not eight [53]. So personal z-scores plus one or two pre-registered features — logistic regression on a binary bad-morning outcome, or Bayesian updating of a single threshold — and defer interactions. Before any of that, pre-tune offline against the Walch dataset: free, labelled, PSG-referenced, same two channels [16]. It also calibrates expectations: 100 − 59.6 = ~40% of genuine wake epochs are missed even by a trained neural net (my arithmetic from the published specificity [15]), so a detector catching 2 of 5 real arousals is normal.

|---|---|---|
| Before night 1 | 0 | Watch fixes (poll interval, precision, HR min/max/n, `h.history`, event lines, firmware version). Window button-press logging (2.30) and the +2 min KSS screen (2.31). `nightly.py` and the JSONL schema. Diary +30 min rating and the free-day boolean. Detector scored against the Walch dataset. |
| First 2 weeks | 1-14 | No randomisation — instrument and observe. Measure the HRM battery cost and the **detector base rate**, by running `--simulate` on every night and reporting how often each trigger would have fired and how early. First SRI at night 8; 14-day baselines for debt, resting HR, onset SD and the wake anchor. SCD41 in from night 1 if it has arrived. |
| First month | 15-40 | Randomise detection vs fixed fade with restricted alternation; ≥10 mornings per arm means a first look at ~25 nights — a plumbing check, not a result. Add the clock proxy and Walch's HR feature. Fit a personal temperature/CO2 curve. |
| After | 40-70+ | ~70 randomised nights is the realistic full run for a modest effect. Add the sunrise arm (factorial, or nested in fixed-fade). Build respiration from the accelerometer; validate `h.history` RMSSD against a chest strap on nights with the Pi logger off. |

One caution on the whole plan: the trial closest to this system found no overall benefit [7], and nobody has published a validation of any consumer smart alarm against PSG or morning alertness [6]. If that is right, these randomised nights would be among the only evidence anywhere — a reason to run the experiment properly, and not to tune thresholds against a 20-night diary.
